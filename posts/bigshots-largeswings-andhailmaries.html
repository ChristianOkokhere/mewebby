Big Shots, Large Swings, and Hail Marys

Apr 25, 2026

A while back I remember sitting agog listening to Travis Kalanick announce Atoms on TBPN. For a full hour, I listened to a guy explain how he worked undercover for years building out his City Storage Systems empire, and how he decided to take this massive bet on a future where delivery and grocery costs even out almost ten years in the future. Truly crazy stuff. The whole time I couldn't help but think, "fuckers don't build like that anymore", or at least it feels that way:

So, I did some digging and it turns out that people tuly more explict about their world domination plots upfront. In Amazons first shareholder letter, Bezos told investors the company would "make bold rather than timid investment decisions.

That statement feels almost moot now because Amazon is a winning buisnesses and its hard to picture the now Jeff Bezos saying anything different, but in 1997, this was not at all the obvious thing to say. At the time Amazon sold books, online. The company had steep competition, brittle infrastructure, and a market that did not yet know what the internet would become. If you went and asked you're local bookstore owner what they would do in a similar situation facing almost certain upheval, they would tell you IDK we're gonna have to find a way to sell more books.

That distinction matters.

You would be hard pressed to find a truly great companies of the last technological cycle that began as agreeable software buisnesses, they each began as attempts to own a future that did not exist yet. Reguadless of who exactly comes to mind, great companies where predicated on the belife that a markets can be remade, supported by economic structures that allowed for niche software solutions to achieve outlier outcomes.

The current crop of founders do not seem to found like that anymore, and Instead of ushering in the golden age of value creation, AI has placed a cloud over our ability to see beyond the present moment. AI has not made ambition impossible, but it has made smaller ambition defensible.

For the first time, its is incredibly rational, profitable, and even admirable to take on capital to build a startup whose ceiling is visable from the begginning. Totaly relaxing the previous baries to value creating has created a stong facsimile for porgress.

The Fog of War

The late 90s and early 2000s was truly the wild west for software companies. As an investor if you came me saying that you could distribute pet products over the internet, at scale, drooling over the opprotunity id probably write you a check for around 10.5million dollars thinking I got the deal of a lifetime.(RIP pets.com, and Bowman Capital).

Durring this time period the sheer burden of creating technology allowed for specilazation to be a defensible moat for distribution. By owning the whole technological verticle, coralling the strongest talent, or plainly solving extremely difficult problems, many startups thrived by owning a singular wedge in a particular market.

It truly was a hurculean effort to take on a market incombant because as a founder literally all of the odds where actually stacked against you. This reality brithed the SAAS buisness and lead to companies forming an export one import many relationship with technology. People just bought alot of software.

AI has largley relaxed these key constraints. It is now becoming the case that:

    1. Traditional buyers (Tech companies) are now well positioned to build bespoke tools inhouse, closing the arbartage opprotunity building niche used to provide.
    2. Traditional moats like difficulty, uniquness, ect. no longer even provide short term insulation from encombants or competetors to search for PMF.

I do admit that if you are a founder, or are looking to start your own thing the above framing is in stark contrast to the current dogma surrounding AI tooling. I am a frim believer that this is the most equiped that the average person has ever been to take on the world.

People have access to better models, tooling, rappid means of distribution, so now its higly unlikley that you accidentially found a buisness like pets.com. Ideas, ventures, products that once took significant investments to spin up can now be prototyped, and validated within days. The cost of creation, and the access to inteligence have collapsed.

This new sense of empowered optionality raises new questions about what is actually worth building. There is an enthraling sense of urgecny that comes you can can solve any problem. Every workflow begins to look like a startup. Every annoyance becomes a product, and every product has an obvious buyer. This is not nothing. Many of these endevaors will become useful. Some will be profitable. Some will sell quickly and make everyone involed look rational, but usefulness is not consequence. A company can work and still not matter. It takes a cetain disposition to build seomthing for a word that is not yet ready.

Humanism as a moat

In a world where seemingly every day more and more aspsects of our live are becoming automated, the next generation of important companies will become more human and not less.

If inference becomes cheap, and software contiunes to become more abundant, value creation will shift towards what remains scarce. Care remains scarce. Trust remains scarce, Belonging remains scarce. Emotional resonance remains scarce.

This is counterintutive because most concersations about AI are framed around replacement. What jobs get replace? Which workflows get automated? Which costs get slashed? The more interesting and potential profitable question may be what cheap inteligence allows us to restore.

Historically, high-touch service was expensive because humman attention was expensive. Think tuttors, concierges, stylists, coaches, care coordinatiors, etc these where all priced as luxary goods. Most people are did not have access to deeply personalized experiences because the labor economics did not work.

AI changes those economics. It makes it possible to scale experiences that feel personal, adaptive, and attentive without requiring one person to sit behind each interaction. In practice I don't think this mean replaceing human texture in non human services, rather I imagine that the best companies will be able to provide human texture at scale.

This concept becomes even more interesting when it is applied to the way that companies are strucutred. Traditionally if you worked at say Google or Meta value creation was sustained mainly at the technology level, meaning that often time the highes ROI individuals where people building the software(aside). In a world where technologoy is less of a fucus roles that are customer facing garner a premium. We are already seeing early ramifications of this shift. Many young companies have smaller software teams, use Forward Deployed Engineers to scale technological inovations, or leverage promises of support and strong partnership to sweaten deals.

The Permission to Act, Surface Area

Ciloqually, surface area refers to the total area that an object occupies. Surface area here refers to the right to expand without seeming random. This isn't just a company's ability to sell mor thinsg. It speaks more to the customer's willingness to let the company matter in more places.

In the future, high surface area will refer to companies that have high levels of permission with their customers. Permission to hold context. Permission to touch sensitive parts of people lives or business function. Permission to make recomendations. Permission to rout work. Permission to transact. Permission to become an interface over their lives.

In the old SaaS world, a company could become large by owning a narrow workflow extremely well. In the AI world, that narrow workflow is more fragile. If the company has no permission to expand, it can be copied, bundled, or absorbed. But if the company owns trust, context, distribution, data, or the system of record, then each new product does not feel like a new product. It feels like the natural next thing the customer wanted it to do anyway.

Technology that touches people’s lives directly is inherently defensible because the value is no longer contained in the software alone. It lives in the relationship between the company and the customer: the context it holds, the trust it earns, the actions it is allowed to take, and the experiences it is able to create.

Final Toughts
