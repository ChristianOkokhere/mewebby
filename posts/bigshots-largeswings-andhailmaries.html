Big Shots, Large Swings, and Hail Marys

Apr 25, 2026

Founders don't found like they used too, or at least it feels that way:

In Amazons first shareholder letter, Bezos told investors the company would "make bold rather than timid investment decisions.

That statement feels trivial now because Amazon is a winning buisnesses, but in 1997, this was not at all the obvious thing to say. At the time, Amazon was an online bookstore with losses, steep competition, brittle infrastructure, and a market that did not yet know what the internet would become. The safe, local decision would have been to build a better way to buy books, instead Bezos bet that commerce itself was about to be reorganized.

That distinction matters.

The great companies of the last technological cycle did not begin as agreeable software buisnesses, they began as attempts to own a future that did not exist yet. Amazon was not a bookstore. Tesla was not a car company. Airbnb was not a way to rent a room. Each of these comapnies were predicated on the belife that a markets could be remade, supported by economic structures that allowed for niche software solutions to achieve outlier outcomes.

The current crop of founders do not seem to found like that anymore, and Instead of ushering in the golden age of value creation, AI has seemingly placed a cloud over our ability to see beyond the present moment. AI has not made ambition impossible, but it has made smaller ambition defensible.

For the first time, its is incredibly rational, profitable, and even admirable to take on capital to build a comapny whose ceiling is visable from the begginning. Totaly relaxing the previous baries to value creating has created a stong facsimile for porgress, barring people from the outlier outcomes that make creating something new truly exciting.

The AI Fog of War

In the late 90s and early 2000s, the sheer burden of creating technology allowed for specilazation to be a defensible moat for distribution. By owning the whole technological verticle, coralling the strongest talent, or plainly solving extremely difficult problems, many startups thrived by owning a singular wedge in a particular market.

It truly was a hurculean effort to take on a market incombant because as a founder literally all of the odds where actually stacked against you (ASIDE, prob still the case alot of the time type shit). This reality brithed the SAAS buisness and lead to companies forming an export one import many relationship with technology. People just bought alot of software.

AI has largley relaxed these key constraints. It is now becoming the case that:

    1. Traditional buyers (Tech companies) are now well positioned to build bespoke tools inhouse, closing the arbartage opprotunity building niche used to provide.
    2. Traditional moats like difficulty, uniquness, ect. no longer even provide short term insulation from encombants or competetors to search for PMF.

I do admit that if you are a founder, or are looking to start your own thing the above framing is in stark contrast to the current dogma surrounding AI tooling. I am a frim believer that this is the most equiped that the average person has ever been to take on the world.

People have access to better models, tooling, rappid means of distribution, the list goes on and on. Ideas, ventures, products that once took significant investments to spin up can now be prototyped, and validated within days. The cost of creation, and the access to inteligence have collapsed.

This is what the future of biz looks like

When you can can solve any problem every workflow begins to look like a startup, every annoyance becomes a product, and every product has an obvious buyer. This is not nothing. Many of these endevaors will become useful. Some will be profitable. Some will sell quickly and make everyone involed look rational.

This is a strong deviation from the SaaS paradigm that shaped the last twenty years of technological value creation.

The death of SaaS

This model represents the modern economy, large companies that focus on capturing market share export one or a set of services while importing all associated technology required to maintian that vision.

This export one, import many relationship only existed because both the cost of copying and the barriers to compete where atronomically high. AI relaxes both of these key constraints.

But usefulness is not consequence. A company can work and still not matter. It takes a cetain disposition to build seomthing for a word that is not yet ready.

So what will the future of value creation look like ___ need to make this sexy? There are two axioms that will make companies succeful:

1. The next genreation of companies will not be defined by the narrowness of thier software wedge, but by the breadth of the surface aread they are allowed to act on.

Surface Area

2. If infrence becomes cheap, and software becomes abundant, then value moves towards things that are scarce: care, identity, belonging, trust, and emotional resonance.
